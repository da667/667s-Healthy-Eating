## *Homemade Chili*

This recipe is another one of my favorites. Its very versatile depending on whether or not you want to add the additional vegetables to the mix -- corn, bell peppers, onion, celery, etc. Additionally, the corn and/or bell peppers do not need to be fresh, frozen can be used, and dumped directly into the pot without thawing them.
Also, this recipe calls for using dry red kidney beans to get the most nutrients, with the least salt possible. However, If you can find them at your grocery store, opt for "no salt added" canned kidney beans to reduce the prep time considerably.
You lose the ability to flavor your beans, but you gain valuable time. Using the no salt added beans makes this an easier recipe to throw into a slow cooker before work, an instant pot after work, or just a dutch oven in the stove, or regular large pan on your stove top much easier, and much faster to finish.

- Prep time: 20 minutes to 1 full night before (If using dry red beans, beans will be best quality if you soak them overnight, and boil them the next day with appropriate seasoning and spices. But this means you will need **at least 8 hours to soak the beans overnight, and 1-2 hours to boil them before cooking your chili**)
- Cook time: ~45 minutes to 1 hour or more (depending on how "low and slow" you want to cook your chili)
- Servings: Many.
- Difficulty: Easy if you use canned beans, Difficult if you want to use dry kidney beans due to time necessity.
- **Health Warning:** This meal is very high in potassium. If you have to manage your potassium, eat this meal very sparingly, or limit your portions!

### Preparing dry red kidney beans for use with your chili
If you opt to use dry kidney beans, it is highly recommend that you soak them overnight, and cook them **before** adding them to your chili. Many will tell you that they can just be cooked for a longer period along with your chili, but **as always, they are wrong.**

Empty the kidney beans into a sufficiently large bowl or pot. Add water, and ensure all kidney beans are immersed. Soak for at least 6-8 hours.
After soaking, boil beans for 1-2 hours, or until sufficiently tender
Optional, but recommended: drain after 3-4 hours of soaking, and refill with water. Drain water before boiling beans, and replace with fresh water.
Optional, but recommended: consider adding spices and/or additional vegetables to beans while they are boiling, as desired: garlic, cumin, chili powder, cayenne pepper, bay leaves, onion powder, celery, carrots, onions

### Instructions
1. **Step 1:** Empty all ingredients **except the meat** into cooking vessel of choice: Slow cooker, Dutch oven, Pot/Pan, Oven-Safe baking pan/pot, etc. Note: If using frozen corn/peppers
2. **Step 2:** Cook ground turkey, ground beef, ground pork, **or** meatless alternative. Season with recommend spices in the Ingredient list above. I recommend splitting any of your ingredients with sodium **half in the sauce** and **half in the meat, but only after the meat has been drained of fat and grease to avoid wasting it.**
3. **Step 3:** add cooked meat to cooking vessel of choice. If cooking on stove, cook over **low** flame ~45 minutes. If cooking in oven, Bake at 375 degrees Fahrenheit, ~1 hour. If slow cooking, slow cook ingredients on "low" setting at least 4 hours, time permitting. In each instance, stir and check every 20-30 minutes if possible to avoid sticking/charring. 
4. **Finish & serve:** Serve in a bowl. Goes incredibly well with shredded cheese, crushed no salt added tortilla chips, and/or greek yogurt (sour cream substitute) or actual sour cream.

### Notes & Tips
Everything about this recipe is adjustable. 
- Add vegetables if you want (carrots, celery, etc.)
- remove vegetables if you want (e.g. use less beans if you are very potassium sensitive)
- Replace the v8 with two cups of vegetable stock if you'd like to reduce sodium even further
- Replace the meat with meatless alteratives, or just don't bother with it all, if you want a completely vegetarian solution.
- Seasoning salt can be substituted with a tsp of low sodium better than bouillion chicken or beef. That'll set you back about 510mg sodium, which is slightly less than the seasoning salt, and salt/pepper/garlic combo seasoning.
This meal, by the nature of its ingredients is extremely high in potassium. a single serving alone will net you 3/4ths of the daily recommended value (for men, this is ~3300 mg per day), so be very careful, if you are potassium sensitive. Consider using less beans if necessary, as the beans are the highest potassium item in the recipe. Using two cans of no salt added canned kidney beans is 3010mg potassium, as opposed to 10 servings of (3/4ths bag) of beans, which will be 4200mg of potassium
- However its also very rich in fiber and protein, also by design.
This meal reheats and stores incredibly well!
