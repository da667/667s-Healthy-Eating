## *Homemade Chili*

This recipe is another one of my favorites. Its very versatile depending on whether or not you want to add the additional frozen corn and/or bell peppers or not. Additionally, the bell peppers do not need to be fresh, either. 
You can buy a bag of frozen green bell peppers, or if you actually enjoy onions, many grocery stores carry a combination of frozen, diced multi-colored bell peppers and onions. Personally I'm onion-averse, so I utilize onion powder instead.
Also, this recipe calls for using dry red kidney beans to get the most nutrients, with the least salt possible. However, If you can find them at your grocery store, opt for "no salt added" canned kidney beans to reduce the prep time considerably.
You lose the ability to flavor your beans, but you gain valuable time. Using the no salt added beans makes this an easier recipe to throw into a slow cooker before work, an instant pot after work, or just a dutch oven in the stove, or regular large pan on your stove top much easier, and much faster to finish.

### Preparing dry kidney beans for this recipe
However, if you want a truly great experience, **use dry beans, and start preparing them the night before.** Let those dry beans soak in a healthy amount of water (at least enough to submerge them) overnight, for at least 8 hours. Althought this is entirely optional, after a few hours, drain the water, and refill it. 
Then, on the next day, you'll want to boil the beans (after changing the water again) for about 1-2 hours. **The best way to determine if the beans are done is via taste test. If they are acceptably soft, they are done.** Always remember that you'll be cooking these beans even more in your pot of chili,
so keep that in mind if they are a little firm.

I recommend boiling the beans with some onion powder, garlic powder, cumin, oregano, chili powder, cayenne, and 1-2 bay leaves, and the resultant beans will be irresistable. You may also optionally choose to boil the beans with diced carrots, onions, and/or celery for more nutrients and flavor as well.
I wouldn't add more than a pinch of salt to the beans while boiling (less than 1/8th of a teaspoon) -- you'll want to add your salt and/or salt mixed spices to tomato sauce that will serve as the base of your chili recipe, and/or the meat, after it has been browned and drained, to avoid draining off the salt flavor 
with the fat and grease.

When I first tried my hand at boiling and preparing dry beans like this, I used 3/4ths of a bag of walmart's "small red beans", and the remaining quarter I put into a ziploc freezer bag with no issues to report.The beans will freeze and preserve well.


- Prep time: 20 minutes to 1 full night before (If using dry red beans, beans will be best quality if you soak them overnight, and boil them the next day with appropriate seasoning and spices. But this means you will need **at least 8 hours to soak the beans overnight, and 1-2 hours to boil them before cooking your chili**)
- Cook time: ~45 minutes to 1 hour or more
- Servings: Many
- Difficulty: Easy if you use canned beans, Difficult if you want to use dry kidney beans due to time necessity.
