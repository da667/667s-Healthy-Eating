## *Recipe Name*

This recipe has been in my family for years now. I can't really attribute this recipe to a source. There are a ton of variations you can try, depending on your dietary requirements.
Please be aware, that, this meal will feature a lot of tomato-based products (diced tomatoes, tomato sauce, v8 and/or vegetable stock), and therefore will be **very** rich in potassium

- Prep time: 1 hour
- Cook time: 1.5 - 2 hours
- Servings: 6 (1 bell pepper + 1/6th portion of sauce)
- Difficulty: Medium, requires a lot of prep and cook time. This is very likely a "weekend" meal, or a meal for WFH warriors!

### Ingredients
- 6 whole bell peppers
  - Any color!
  - Green is usually cheaper, but you'll want to cook it longer to bring out its flavor.
  - Multi-colored will be sweeter and offer unique flavors
- 1 lb ground meat
  - lean turkey is the healthiest
  - chicken, beef, or pork or vegan/vegetarian substitutes are all acceptable depending on your dietary needs/restrictions
- 1 bag frozen, microwaveable riced cauliflower
- 2 cans of No salt Added Diced Tomatoes
  - Petite sliced, or regular sliced.
  - Its tempting to go for the garlic basic or fire roasted varieties, but **beware: they are much higher in sodium.**
  - You can always try dicing fresh tomatoes... if you have all the time in the world. 2-3 Standard Beefsteak tomatoes should work
- 29-32oz (1 big can, or four 8oz cans) of No Salt Added Tomato Sauce
- 16 oz of Reduced Sodium v8
  - Can be entirely substituted with No Salt Added Vegetable stock, to give yourself options to use more salt in your seasoning
- 8 oz of No Salt Added Vegetable stock
- A wide array of spices to suit your tastes and/or sodium restrictions. Here are a few that I recommend for this dish:
  - Smoked Paprika, or Chili Powder (if you want a tiny kick)
  - Onion Powder
  - Garlic Powder
  - Cayenne Pepper (If you want to bring the heat up a little bit more)
  - Seasoning Salt, or reduced sodium Better than Boullion Chicken/Beef, or substitute with your own salt-included or no-salt added seasoning mix (e.g. montreal steak seasoning, Salt/Pepper/Garlic mix, or Mrs. Dash equivalent)
  - Fresh garlic if you REALLY wanna be fancy
    - Peeled whole -- just toss the pieces in
    - Roasted
    - Or just plain minced
    - Jar minced garlic _also_ works if you're lazy, like me.

### Instructions
1. **Step 1:** Prepare large baking pan, or dutch oven, and pre-heat oven to 400 degrees Fahrenheit
2. **Step 2:** Prepare microwavable cauliflower rice according to package (e.g. 6 minutes in 1000 watt microwave)
3. **Step 3:** While cauliflower coooks, prepare whole bell peppers by slicing off the top of the bell pepper, cutting out its core, removing its seeds, and as much of the white interior rind as you can, then rinse.
   - Optionally, if you want to keep the "lid of the pepper, you can cut out the center stem portion from the top of the bell pepper and cook that as well.
4. **Step 4:** Mix cauliflower rice, and raw ground meat into mixing bowl.
   - **Warning: Please allow time for the cauliflower rice to cool before mixing with ground meat to avoid scalding**
   - While mixing, consider adding a portion of the seasoning and spices you have prepared for your meal.
     - I recommend **minimizing** the use of your salt/sodium spices For the pepper stuffing. You'll want to save what salt/sodium you can use for the sauce, since we are use a variety of no salt added sauces.
5. **Step 5:** Stuff your prepared bell peppers evenly with the meat and cauliflower rice mixture
6. **Step 6:** In the baking pan or dutch oven you'll be using, dump all of the wet ingredients together -- diced tomatoes, tomato sauce, v8, and vegetable stock. Add seasonings and spices
   - Go absolutely ham with your spices here! As they say, measure with your heart!
   - **Note:** If you sample the sauce before cooking it, you'll think that its bland and bitter. I know the temptation is strong, but do not put too much salt or too many spices into the sauce! The flavor of the sauce will mellow out as it cooks, and your spicy ingredients will becom more pronounced as the sauce boils away a bit.
7. **Step 7:** Submerge stuffed peppers into the sauce, coating evenly. Using a serving spoon, ladle sauce over the top of the stuffed pepper to completely coat each stuffed pepper. Bake for 1.5 - 2 hours. Check peppers every 30-45 minutes, ladling more sauce over the top of the peppers to ensure the mixture within retains moisture.
   - The longer the sauce is cooked, the more it will boil down, and the flavors will concentrate to bring out all the spices and umami you put in.
   - If you elect to cook the sauce for a shorter period of time, The sauce will be thinner, and the spices not quite so strong. Cook to your preference
   - **Note:** In my experience, 1.5-2 hours is the amount of time it takes to cause the sauce to concentrate the flavor **and** how long it takes to make the bell peppers able to be penetrated/split open effortlessly with a spoon or fork for easiest consumption
8. **Finish & serve:** Food will be very hot. Enjoy!

### Notes & Tips
- There are a great many things you can do to change this meal up. Depending on your dietary needs, you can bump up the salt/umami or seasoning as much, or as little as your dietary needs and tastes dictate.
- The sauce can be prepared and mimic/emulate spaghetti sauce if you like a sweeter, more savory red sauce or can mimic chili if you like things hot!
- Depending on how many you're feeding, results in tons of leftovers, or an entirely empty dutch oven
- Leftovers reheat **extremely well**
- Do you have an excessive amount of sauce left over? Save it in a microwave safe bowl. The sauce on its own reheats really well, and can serve as a great, relatively savory or spicy tomato soup, or even a makeshift spaghetti sauce!
- If you aren't a fan of riced cauliflower, the riced cauliflower can be replaced with any type of rice you desire. I typically use ~3/4ths to 1 full cup of brown rice, if I'm not in a cauliflower mood
  - **Very Important: Cook the rice before mixing with the ground meat of your choice. The meat/juices will NOT provide enough moisture to cook the rice in the oven!**
- If you really want to be creative, I had a variation of this meal, called stuffed pepper soup at a restaurant in northern Michigan called the "45th parallel". Do you want to turn this into a soup as well? Then try this:
  - Finely dice all of the bell peppers allocated for your meal.
  - evenly distribute the meat/cauliflower rice mixture through the sauce, along with the diced bell peppers
  - At the 30-45 minute intervals, stir the contents, breaking up excessively large chunks of the meat/cauliflower (or meat/rice) mixture.
  - Enjoy!
- **Warning: Many of the ingredients in this meal are extremely rich in potassium. Those suffering from Hyperkalemia (too much potassium) should be extremely careful!**
